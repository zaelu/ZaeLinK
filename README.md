An unique, open-source VR Gunstock project. 


The ZaeLink VR Gunstock is a gunstock that starts from Lateral Thinking (sic) paradigm that differs from most of already existing VR Gunstocks. 
The paradigm is defined by:

**Fix the pains of gunstocks** in an Open Source manner and Cheap to build personally or commercially with a leaning on Function over Form, Competition Performance over Traditional Looks and drawing inspiration from AK47 origin, - Lose Tolerances playing in its advantages, The philosophy behind its design and construction follows 10 core **"First Principles"** plus a motto.

The motto is:   **Everybody SHOULD build a prototype!**

This is a play on a quote of Elon Musk that said something on the lines: "Anyone can build a prototype but building a product is way more difficult". 
We believe there is some truth in that saying but also we can spot an opposite and glorious route . We cannot always wait for someone to solve all problems and we just buy the solutions as products. We, each one of us on this Planet,  need to actively search problems and find solutions for them even if that means to have a world of Prototypes.

The First principles at the core of ZaeLink VR gunstock creation are:

### 1. it should connect cvasi automatically while blindfolded. 
### 2. it should have strong and reliable connection. 
### 3. it should easily disconnect on command only.
### 4. it should stay away from user operations with the hands and not interfere with the tracking at all.
### 5. it should be adjustable and personnalisable in few moves and seconds while blindfolded.
### 6. it has to be cheap and have few materials in BoM  and be easy to print and build.
### 7. It has to work with all virtual weapons and activities in VR shooters type games.  And yes.. it has to work with pump action shotguns too... and it does. 
### 8. It should work with all VR headsets or most common of them at least. Users can adapt it for future ones easily.
### 9. It needs not to be dangerous or heavy. So it doesn't look like a weapon. It has no exposed metallic parts, no screws, no visible magnets, no unnecessary sharp edges and as many round shapes as possible and be as minimalistic as possible (depending on configuration).
### 10. It needs to work with VR games and their specific style and not to necessary it needs not to look like a specific real life weapon but more like Olympic sports ones i.e. specialized in performance in games not robustness under real life war fire.

**What do we mean by automatic connection?**

This main principle is a condition sine qua non for a functional VR Gunstock. We literally have some Gunstocks today that hang from our necks and expect us to connect both handles/controllers to it in the heat of the battle while blind folded. We find this wrong and one of the main reasons people don't use them. Nobody assembles a weapon fractions of a second before a firefight... blind folded.
It also means it should be effortless. You don't focus at all on connecting the gunstock, you focus on playing. You grab the virtual  weapon with right hand, then you grab it with the left hand and immediately aim or start playing and the connection is done automatically and the gunstock is locked in position without any other intervention. To disconnect you just start to turn the two halves of the Magnetic Core one over the other and then pull away the off han

**What does it mean to work with specifics of VR first person shooters?**

Think more on how a Olympic gun looks than a specific weapon from real military. It's why it allows playing while holding it not like a real gun is held if user wishes to. Meaning it can be held centrally on the chest not necessary as a weapon on the side with the head tilted on it but if user wants both ways can be used.

**What headsets are supported?**

Currently working with Quest 3/3S Pico 4/4Ultra and Quest 2. Planned nex is the Steam Frame.

**What does "work with all headsets" mean?**
Even if the handles change from one headset to the other they modify minimally their grips and the Gustock remains virtually the same on all platforms. When all things are considered it makes so there is no need for player to adapt to it but rather the gunstock adapts to player.

**Where it will be available?**
All 3D stl hosting sites and GitHub under CERN Open Hardware [LICENCE](LICENSE).

## 🔩 Bill of Materials (BOM)
To build this, you will need the following non-printed parts:
* PLA 400-700 grams depending on how many parts and types are printed
* 10 to 16 Magnets 15x6mm button magnets without any sockets or fixtures or screws and screw holes!!! Just simple button magnets. They can be thinner like 3mm if their power needs to be lowered. Or thicker for opposite effect. In such cases the lids need some modification to accommodate te changed thickness of the magnets.
* Double sided duck tape or scotch. Very thin one and cheap. It's role is important as it creates the necessary clutch like behavior of the adjusting ball socket joints!
* 3 M4x40 screws and 3 self locking nuts for the tightening knobs for the variable lenght connecting rods
* SUPER GLUE or other better PLA glue (some rare substances started to exist). Very important how it is applied for strength of the balls on top of connecting rods. Recommended a small bottle of 5g that has a brush like applicator!!! Don't cheap out here for the usual 1-2g squeezable tubes as they are messy and render worse results. :D

## 🛠️ Assembly Instructions (to be added)
 1. Print the 3D parts from the STL files. Follow the detailed instructions about best way to print them so you get strong and nice parts.
 2. Mount the magnets in the core halves. Don't rush here. Be careful not to let the magnets snap from far!! They can crack!! The magnets are strong and have tendency to go where you didn't intended. Look at instruction video on how to build for small details but it should be like placing coins in round holes only these coins are alive and funny. 
There are 2 main ways (patterns) you can assemble them:
Olympic with 6 magnets on one side grouped 3 up facing North polarity to the second Core Halve and 3 down facing South polarity in the direction of the second halve and for the second halve there are only 4 magnets placed on pairs facing in opposite directions magnetically speaking and separated by one empty place. Look either on instructional video or in description file for more info.
Fragnetto (which is the simpler one) with 6 magnets facing all same direction magnetically in one halve and 6 magnets in the second half likewise facing same direction magnetic polarity wise but attracting to the other half. I.e. 6 facing North towards 6 facing South.
After doing the first halve side, best practice is to place the second halve in the normal ( i.e. not rotated for reloading or detaching) position on top and now use the attraction between magnets to snap into position the six other magnets from second half. Don't rotate the halves until you have secured the lids on top as the magnets will have the tendency to rotate also in their places when you rotate the halves against each other. The lids lock the magnets from that in place rotation.
 3. Assemble the connecting rods (fixed or with variable length). For this step, is very important to use Super Glue to glue the balls on the rods end but with ATTENTION to few details:
- First you should carefully print the balls as provided tilted to have them in maximum toughness. They are printed separately because the rods would not be strong if printed in one go and also locking the knobs are impossible to print in place without being super weak. So after printing, clean the parts carefully. Make sure there are no sharp edges on the lip of the tube of the balls so it doesn't act like a guillotine that cuts into the rod itself. Like wise no sharp edges on the rods on the parts that enter the balls. Use a deburring tool for this. Very cheap on Allyexpress.
- Rough a bit the part of the rods that enter the balls to increase adherence. Do the same for inside of the balls. Just rough them a bit don't thin them. They should fit snuggly.
- Apply superglue on the end of the rod and a bit inside the ball. Place the Locking Knob on the ball part !! WARNING !! careful with its orientation! This is CRITICAL! If you mount it in wrong direction and you glue it in place it's done! You need to redo the rod, break the wrong one and recover the knob and pay attention next time! The thread opening of the Knob should be towards the ball! Later an embossed arrow will be added on the Knob to point towards the Ball but still use common sense and pay good attention to this step. I am talking from experience :D .
- Press the ball on the rod as good as possible. It should fit all the way in. The bottom of the hole in the ball should connect with the rod's end for best result. If air gaps are left they might provide a place to brake the ball neck during use!! Let the glue dry before use and do one ball-rod end at a time!! There are only 6 to do so don't rush!
 4. Apply a square (yes don't bother with round patches is not important) of double sided duck tape directly on the ball's end. After placing it and pressing it to fit the roundness of the ball... doesn't matter it makes kinks! you can remove the covering paper to expose the other glued side of the double sided ducktape Now is ready to go into socket.
 5. Place the ball on the socket and align the rod as best as possible with the final position you want it to be so you don't need to turn it too much afterwards! Tighten it with the Locking Nut as best as possible. Lock the Locking Nut with a small 5cm long piece of normal filament that was never passed through extruder gears so it doesn't break when bent! The filament needs to be folded in two so it looks like a "U". Make sure some of the holes in the nut align with some of the holes in the core (or later, controller cups or buttstock end) and pass the U shaped filament through 2 of the holes from nut end towards the piece you are connecting it to. Now t Locking Nut is itself Locked from turning.
 6. Now you can adjust the ball socket joint. Do it with smallest necessary force and by placing your hands firmly as close as possible to articulation. You want small moves not large moves that put pressure on the whole assembly or try to break the rods!!!!!!!!! The force required should be great enough to be more than the force required to detach the Magnetic Core halves!! meaning the mag Core should open before the gunstock starts to deform or readjust in the adjusting rods! To achieve that you need to check the tightness of the locking knobs is sufficient. If not, remove the locking pins... the U shaped filament wires and tighten some more and make sure after that the holes in the nut are still aligned with the holes in the part! Resecure the nut with U filament and adjust again. Best way to really check this is in game after a bit of play and the Gunstock is warm from hands a bit. When is cold the locking happens earlier than when is warm!
